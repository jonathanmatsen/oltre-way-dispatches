The Andaz Epilogue: Or, How a Missed Flight Became the Best Part

On standby karma, sauna diplomacy, Sancerre at the Faena, and the particular peace of a hotel beach on a Tuesday morning.

Jonathan • The Oltre Way February 8–10, 2026

The flight was at 5 PM and I didn't make it. Simple as that. Standby giveth, standby taketh. The same system that got me here for almost nothing on a Friday red-eye looked at my Sunday departure and said no. I stood at the gate, still running on whatever fumes remain after a sunrise at Space and four hours of sleep, and watched the plane push back without me.

The responsible move would have been to find a seat on the 6 AM Monday flight, set an alarm, suffer through it. Instead I opened the Hyatt app, typed "Miami Beach," and booked two nights at the Andaz on points. Twenty-five thousand a night. Cash rate: seven hundred dollars. I wasn't going home yet.

The Uber was twenty minutes late. Then it showed up as a black SUV. Free upgrade. The most Miami sequence possible: chaos followed by an inexplicable luxury escalation. I climbed in, gave the address, and closed my eyes for the fifteen-minute ride to Collins Avenue.

I should say something about the Andaz because it deserves it. This is not a points hotel in the way that phrase usually lands. It's brand new, art deco bones with a modern intervention that actually works, and José Andrés is running everything you eat and drink. Bar Centro in the lobby. Aguasal on the ground floor. The Bazaar coming soon. Two pools, beach service, orange and white umbrellas on the sand two minutes from your room. It's the kind of place that would be genuinely cool at full price and happens to also be bookable with points, which is the exact sweet spot where strategic and luxurious meet.

• • •

Monday, 11:30 AM — I woke up wrecked. The weekend catching up all at once. And there was a work deliverable due. A critical one. No extensions, no excuses. The problem: my laptop charger was in Utah. Left it at my sister's house during the ski trip the week before. My laptop had been running on battery since Salt Lake City. It was dead.

So I finished the report in Excel on my iPhone. Lying in bed at the Andaz Miami Beach, squinting at a spreadsheet on a five-inch screen, formatting cells with my thumbs. It was tedious and absurd and I got it done and hit send and then I went directly to the sun.

The spa attendant let me into the sauna for free. Globalist thing, or maybe just Monday-morning kindness. Either way I sat there in the steam and felt the weekend leave my body in layers. Space at 4 AM. The cotton candy sunrise. The missed flight. The black SUV. All of it dissolving into heat and quiet.

Monday, 3:00 PM — Faena Miami Beach. I was supposed to stay at the Andaz all evening. Bar Centro, croquetas, early bed. But my feet took me north on the sand and I ended up at the Faena bar ordering a glass of Sancerre and an empanada. The way you do.

The Faena is a Baz Luhrmann production. There is a gold mammoth by Damien Hirst in the lobby. The interiors are maximalist in a way that would be obscene if they weren't so committed to the vision. It's Belle Époque fever dream meets Miami tropicalia, and sitting at the bar in the late afternoon light with a glass of cold white wine, I understood something I'd been circling for two weeks.

I am so much better at doing life than I feel like sometimes inside. I am a lot more competent and fun and interesting and cool than I give myself credit for. And it's a nice realization.

Not a grand epiphany. Just a quiet noticing. In the past two weeks I'd planned a mixed-ability ski trip across four Utah resorts for three people. Flew standby to Miami solo and built a weekend that went from Pérez Art Museum to a Premier Cru Bordeaux in a courtyard to a sunrise at Space. Finished a work deliverable on my iPhone from a pool chair. Turned a missed flight into two nights at a José Andrés hotel for the price of points I'd have spent on a Hyatt Place in Dallas.

The gap between how competent I actually am and how I sometimes feel about it is worth paying attention to. Because the evidence is pretty overwhelming on one side.

• • •

Monday, 6:00 PM — Walking back to the Andaz along Collins Avenue I passed a construction site at 3425. Steel rising behind fencing. The Aman. Kengo Kuma's 18-story tower, 22 residences, already sold out, opening next year. They're restoring the Versailles Hotel next to it as a 56-room Aman property. Japanese minimalism meeting Miami art deco on the sand, right in the middle of the Faena District.

All along this stretch of Collins, the branded residences are going up. Dolce & Gabbana. Aston Martin. Bentley. Fendi. Cipriani. Forty-five projects from twenty-two brands, either built or in the pipeline. Miami is the global capital of the branded residence after Dubai. The economics are simple: no state income tax attracts wealth, brand licensing lets developers charge 30-50% premiums, buyers get to literally live inside a logo.

There is something hollow about most of it. Living in an Aston Martin tower isn't taste. It's outsourcing your aesthetic to a brand that makes cars. The Faena works because Luhrmann actually designed something with a vision. Most of these towers are just names on buildings.

The Aman is the exception. Aman's identity IS the experience of being in the space. The architecture, the wellness, the intentional quiet. Kengo Kuma's tiered terraces, every unit with ocean views, Japanese joinery meeting art deco curves. That's not a logo play. That's a philosophy built into concrete and glass and sand.

I walked past Miami's future on my way back from a glass of Sancerre. This city is building itself into something nobody fully understands yet.

• • •

Bar Centro was full of conference people when I got back. Monday-evening energy, name tags and networking voices. Not my frequency. I took a shower and got into bed and fell asleep before 9 PM for the first time in a week. The air conditioning hummed. The ocean was right there, two minutes through the lobby and across the sand, doing what it does whether anyone's watching or not.

Tuesday, 10:00 AM — Andaz Beach. The final morning. Orange and white umbrellas. The Atlantic doing its thing. I took a work Zoom from a beach chair, camera off, toes in the sand, then packed up while still on the call. The laptop was dead. The phone was doing everything. It had been doing everything for three days and somehow that was fine.

There's a version of this trip that ends Sunday afternoon on a standby flight home. Three days, tight arc, clean ending. That version is good. But this version is better. The two extra days at the Andaz turned a weekend into something else. The Saturday and Sunday were performance. The Monday and Tuesday were the thing that performance was protecting me from: stillness, sun, the quiet recognition that I'm actually good at this.

Good at what, exactly? All of it. The planning. The pivoting. The knowing when to push through a deliverable on a phone screen and when to walk to the Faena for a glass of wine. The instinct for the right hotel at the right moment for the right price. The ability to turn a missed flight into the best two days of the trip.

The best travel isn't just what you plan. It's what you do with the things that go wrong. A missed flight, a dead laptop, a $700 hotel room that costs you nothing. The whole trip was a masterclass in something I'm only now starting to name.

Checkout was late. Globalist perk. I sat on the pool deck until the last possible moment, the sun on my face, the conference people gone, the beach quiet in the way hotel beaches are quiet on a Tuesday when everyone who was here for the weekend is already home. I didn't want to leave. Nobody ever does from that chair.

• • •

The flight is at 5:30. Turkish Airlines lounge before boarding. There's time for one more hour of this and then the Uber and then the airport and then whatever Miami's TSA has decided to do today, which based on Sunday's evidence will involve thirty minutes of organized chaos even with PreCheck.

Five days in Miami. Three planned, two improvised. Brickell to Mid-Beach. Hyatt Centric to Andaz. Amazonico to Tam Tam to Space to a glass of Sancerre at the Faena to orange umbrellas on a Tuesday morning. The trip kept expanding past its own borders. It kept finding new rooms inside itself.

That's what Miami does, if you let it. It doesn't end when you think it ends. It gives you one more night, one more morning, one more version of itself you weren't expecting. You just have to miss the flight.

I'll be back. I'm always back. It's not even a decision anymore. It's just what February looks like now.
